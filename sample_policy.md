# Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Financing of Terrorism Policy

**Institution:** Meridian Bank Hong Kong Limited (illustrative)
**Version:** 3.2
**Effective date:** 15 March 2025
**Policy owner:** Head of Financial Crime Compliance
**Review cycle:** Annual

---

## 1. Purpose and Scope

This policy sets out Meridian Bank Hong Kong Limited's ("the Bank") approach to preventing money laundering and terrorist financing, in accordance with the Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorist Financing Ordinance (Cap. 615) ("AMLO") and applicable HKMA guidance.

It applies to all staff, contractors, and third-party service providers acting on behalf of the Bank across its Hong Kong operations, including branch, private banking, corporate banking, and digital channels.

## 2. Governance and Accountability

The Board of Directors holds ultimate responsibility for the effectiveness of the Bank's AML/CFT framework. Day-to-day accountability rests with the Head of Financial Crime Compliance, who reports quarterly to the Risk Committee.

The Bank has appointed a Money Laundering Reporting Officer (MLRO) at senior management level. The MLRO is independent of front-office functions and has direct access to the Board.

## 3. Risk-Based Approach

The Bank adopts a risk-based approach to AML/CFT controls, consistent with paragraph 2 of the HKMA Guideline on AML/CFT. Customer, product, geography, and delivery-channel risks are assessed periodically and used to calibrate the depth of due diligence applied.

An institutional-wide ML/TF risk assessment is conducted annually and approved by senior management.

## 4. Customer Due Diligence (CDD)

At onboarding, all customers are subject to identification and verification procedures. The following are collected and verified against reliable, independent sources:

- Full legal name
- Date of birth (for natural persons) or place of incorporation (for legal persons)
- Residential or business address
- Nature of intended relationship
- Beneficial ownership information for legal-person customers

Simplified due diligence may be applied to lower-risk relationships as determined by the risk assessment framework.

Enhanced due diligence is applied to higher-risk customers, including additional information on source of wealth and source of funds.

## 5. Sanctions Screening

All customers, connected parties, and cross-border payment counterparties are screened at onboarding and daily thereafter against:

- United Nations Security Council consolidated sanctions list
- US OFAC SDN list
- EU consolidated sanctions list
- The Bank's internal watchlist

Positive matches are escalated to the Financial Crime Compliance team within one business day for adjudication. Payments flagged for potential sanctions match are held pending clearance.

## 6. Transaction Monitoring

The Bank operates an automated transaction monitoring system that produces alerts based on predefined rules and thresholds. Alerts are triaged by first-line analysts and escalated as necessary.

The transaction monitoring rule set is reviewed annually by the Financial Crime Compliance team.

## 7. Suspicious Transaction Reporting

Where a member of staff forms a knowledge or suspicion that a transaction may involve proceeds of an indictable offence or terrorist property, an internal suspicious transaction report (STR) must be submitted to the MLRO without delay.

The MLRO is responsible for evaluating internal reports and, where appropriate, filing a suspicious transaction report with the Joint Financial Intelligence Unit (JFIU).

## 8. Record-Keeping

Customer identification records are retained for at least five years following termination of the business relationship. Transaction records are retained for at least five years from the date of the transaction.

## 9. Training

All new joiners complete AML/CFT induction training within 30 days of joining. Existing staff complete refresher training annually. Additional targeted training is provided to staff in higher-risk roles.

## 10. Independent Assessment

The Bank's AML/CFT framework is subject to review by Internal Audit on a risk-based cycle. Findings are reported to the Audit Committee.
